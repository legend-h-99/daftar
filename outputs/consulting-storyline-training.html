Use SCR when the story is a change journey

Situation, Complication, Resolution gives the audience a reason to move.

S

Situation

What is true today? Set shared context without arguing.

→
C

Complication

What changed or broke? Introduce tension that requires action.

→
R

Resolution

What should we do? State the answer as a decision.

•
Situation– Current growth is strong, but concentrated in two channels.
•
Complication– CAC rose 38%, making the old channel mix uneconomic.
•
Resolution– Shift spend to partner-led acquisition and pause low-LTV search campaigns.

Use SCQA when the audience needs a sharper question

Situation, Complication, Question, Answer turns ambiguity into a decision frame.

S

Situation

The business has launched successfully.

→
C

Complication

Growth is slowing despite higher spend.

→
Q

Question

Where should leadership focus next?

→
A

Answer

Prioritize retention before more acquisition.

Rule of thumb: If the answer is obvious once the complication is clear, use SCR. If the complication creates several possible choices, use SCQA.

Write the storyline as dot-dash before making slides

Dots are governing thoughts; dashes are the evidence each thought needs.

•
Recommendation– Launch the pilot in one city before scaling nationally.
•
Why it matters– The target segment has urgent pain, but onboarding risk is high.
•
Why this route– A city pilot validates adoption, support load, and unit economics quickly.
•
What to do next– Select users, define measures, and decide scale criteria before launch.

Action titles turn slides into arguments

A slide title should say the conclusion, not the topic.

Topic title

Market overview

This tells the reader what the slide is about, but not what they should conclude.

Action title

The market is large enough to support a focused premium entry

This tells the reader the claim the slide will prove.

Rewrite test: If the title can sit in a table of contents and still tell the whole story, it is probably an action title.

Build the slide skeleton before designing the slide

Skeletons reduce rework because logic and layout are decided together.

•
Action title– The conclusion the slide must prove.
•
Exhibit– The chart, table, process, or comparison that carries the proof.
•
Support notes– Definitions, assumptions, or caveats needed for trust.
•
Implication– The so-what that connects this slide to the next one.

Skeleton output

1. Title claim
2. Main visual
3. Three proof points
4. Footnote or source
5. Transition to next slide

Final checklist: a strong storyline passes five tests

Use this before sharing a draft with leadership.

•
Answer first– The recommendation is visible by slide 2.
•
One idea– Each slide proves only one governing thought.
•
Logical order– Each slide makes the next one feel necessary.
•
Specific titles– Titles are conclusions with verbs, numbers, or direction.
•
Evidence fit– Every exhibit directly proves the action title.
Best final question: If a busy executive read only the titles, would they understand the full recommendation and why it is right?
